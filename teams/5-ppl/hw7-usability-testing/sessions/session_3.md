#### Task-by-task observations

##### Task 1: Sign up

- **What they did:** Read the description of the profile and mistake log, then completed registration without hesitating. Asked whether practice history would be available on another device.
- **What they said out loud:** "If it saves what I miss, having an account makes sense."
- **Completed?** Yes

##### Task 2: Set up the automation

- **What they did:** Selected YouTube and "At most once an hour." Followed the instructions carefully. Reread the explanation of the temporary file before finishing the automation, then checked the return to YouTube.
- **What they said out loud:** "So this stops it from asking me again when I return to YouTube?"
- **Completed?** Yes, without assistance

##### Task 3: One practice question and its explanation (aha moment)

- **What they did:** Answered the absolute-value question incorrectly after overlooking the negative solution. Read the explanation and tagged it "Careless." Opened the profile, looked at the mistake log, and tapped the topic entry trying to reopen the full problem.
- **What they said out loud:** "I want to come back to this exact problem later, not just know the topic."
- **Completed?** Yes

**Hesitations:** Reread the temporary-file explanation during setup.

**Wrong clicks:** Tapped the topic entry in the mistake log expecting it to open the missed problem; it did not.

**Questions out loud:** Asked whether history syncs across devices, whether mistake tags change future questions, and how to retry a missed problem.

**Dropped tasks:** None in the assigned flow. Stopped searching for a retry option after about a minute.

**Workarounds:** None observed.

**Did they reach the aha moment?** Yes, around minute 8, after reading the explanation. Interest increased when the mistake appeared in the profile.

#### Post-session answers

1. What does it do? "Adds small practice sessions and helps me see patterns in what I miss."
2. When did it click? "When the mistake appeared in my profile."
3. Most confusing part? "Whether the mistake categories actually change the questions I get."
4. Anything missing? "An obvious way to retry missed questions."
5. Would you use it again? "Yes, alongside my regular studying."
6. Magic wand? "Build a review session from my previous mistakes."

#### Quick insight

The biggest thing this session showed us was that for a student who already studies consistently, reviewing past mistakes may be the strongest reason to come back.
