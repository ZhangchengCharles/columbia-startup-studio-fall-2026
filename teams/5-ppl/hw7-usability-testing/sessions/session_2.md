# Session 2

**Date:** November 9, 2026  
**Participant:** P2  
**Who they are:** 18-year-old high school senior who regularly dismisses Screen Time limits. Wants to cut down TikTok scrolling but dislikes strict app blockers. Uses an iPhone and has used basic shortcuts before.  
**How we found them:** A classmate's cousin who volunteered through a group chat  
**Stranger or someone we know?** Stranger  
**Consent form signed?** Yes  
**Recording consent?** No (written notes only)  
**Recording link:** None, participant declined recording  
**Moderator:** Team member A  
**Note-taker:** Team member B  
**Session length:** 14 minutes

#### Task-by-task observations

##### Task 1: Sign up

- **What they did:** Looked for a way to try the product before registering. Asked whether an account was required. After learning that an account saves practice history, completed registration.
- **What they said out loud:** "Can I see what it does before I give you my email?"
- **Completed?** Yes, after asking what registration was for

##### Task 2: Set up the automation

- **What they did:** Selected TikTok and first chose "Every time I open it." Reconsidered and changed to "Every third open." Read how to pause questions and how to turn off the automation. Followed the Shortcuts instructions without help.
- **What they said out loud:** "Every single time would get annoying. I'd probably turn it off."
- **Completed?** Yes

##### Task 3: One practice question and its explanation (aha moment)

- **What they did:** Located Skip before reading the question. Asked whether a wrong answer would stop them from returning to TikTok. Chose 40 on the absolute-value question, read the explanation, and tapped "Back to TikTok."
- **What they said out loud:** "Okay, so I don't have to get it right to leave."
- **Completed?** Yes

**Hesitations:** Stopped at the sign-up form to look for a way to try first. Changed frequency setting after first choosing "Every time I open it."

**Wrong clicks:** None observed.

**Questions out loud:** "Can I see what it does before I give you my email?" Asked whether Skip had a penalty and whether a wrong answer locked the app.

**Dropped tasks:** None. Skipped optional mistake tagging.

**Workarounds:** Looked for Skip before reading the question, checking for an exit first.

**Did they reach the aha moment?** Yes, around minute 9, after finishing the question and reading the explanation without being forced to retry.

#### Post-session answers

1. What does it do? "Interrupts the scrolling habit for a second and gives me something useful to do."
2. When did it click? "When I realized I could get a question wrong and still go back."
3. Most confusing part? "The name made me think I had to unlock my app."
4. Anything missing? "A way to choose the hours when it interrupts me."
5. Would you use it again? "Maybe. I'd want to see whether it gets annoying after a few days."
6. Magic wand? "Only show questions during the hours I usually waste time."

#### Quick insight

The biggest thing this session showed us was that the tester became willing once they understood that answering, skipping and pausing were all their choice.
